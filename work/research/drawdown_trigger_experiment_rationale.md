# Drawdown-triggered exposure control: research rationale

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-27  
**Decision:** A small, pre-registered drawdown-trigger experiment is justified as a risk-control challenger. The literature does not establish that it will improve net returns or enforce a hard 20% loss limit in this ETF implementation.

## What the project has already tested

The current screens cover fixed passive NIFTY/Gold exposure, quarterly static mixes, trailing-volatility scaling, an India VIX overlay, and a stationary-bootstrap stress. They do not appear to include a rule that changes the portfolio's risky allocation directly from its own high-water-mark drawdown.

On the common 2015–2026 path, the passive 45% initial NIFTY/Gold mix returned +129.00% after modeled current fees and 50 bps per-side slippage, with a conservative daily OHLC bound of -17.37%. The 60% initial mix returned +172.02% with a -19.23% bound, but its 63-session-block, ten-year bootstrap estimate crossed -20% in 10.00% of paths. The 45% and 60% estimates were 3.58% and 10.00%, respectively. These are retrospective and conditional-resampling results; all dates through 2026-10-23 were already inspected. See [the exposure study](../../outputs/passive_mix_exposure_method.md), [the joint volatility screen](../../outputs/joint_exposure_volatility_method.md), and [the bootstrap design and results](../../outputs/etf_drawdown_bootstrap_method.md).

## What the source literature supports

- **Direct drawdown-control policies are a real portfolio-control family.** Mantilla-García formalizes dynamic allocation between cash and a risky portfolio to keep losses under a chosen floor, and studies absolute, excess, and relative drawdown variants. This supports testing a causal allocation rule, but its theoretical insurance result is not a guarantee for daily ETF trading with gaps, next-open orders, transaction costs, and incomplete cash-flow data. See [Mantilla-García (2014), *Dynamic Allocation Strategies for Absolute and Relative Loss Control*](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2405530).
- **A simple drawdown throttle has practitioner precedent, not proof for this market.** AQR describes a simplified model that combines month-to-date, year-to-date, trailing-one-year, and average-one-year drawdowns; it reduces exposure in 14-percentage-point increments toward a 50% minimum as the measures approach a -10% floor. The example is built on a diversified 67-market, four-asset-class trend portfolio, and the paper explicitly says the simplified model is not an actual AQR portfolio. Use it only to motivate a narrow test, not to import its performance or parameters as validated Indian ETF settings. See [Thapar and Villalon (2019), *Chasing Your Own Tail (Risk), Revisited*](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/AQR-Chasing-Your-Own-Tail-Risk-Revisited.pdf?hash=BF573308185FC897A61E834826EB28C1&sc_lang=en).
- **Volatility scaling evidence is mixed out of sample.** Moreira and Muir's inverse-realized-variance portfolio is the established mechanism behind several existing project screens; its original results are not a direct test of high-water drawdown triggers. Cederburg et al. later find that volatility-managed portfolios do not systematically beat unmanaged portfolios in direct comparisons, and reasonable real-time versions often underperform out of sample. This is a reason to preserve simple controls and demand a frozen forward test, not a reason to infer that a drawdown trigger will fail. See [Moreira and Muir (2017)](https://amoreira2.github.io/alan-moreira.github.io/VolPortfolios_published.pdf) and [Cederburg et al. (2020)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X2030132X).

## Narrow experiment worth running

Use the 60% initial passive mix as the challenger and compare it with the existing fixed 60% and fixed 45% paths. Within the risky sleeve, keep NIFTYBEES and GOLDBEES equally weighted; hold the unused balance in cash. Calculate drawdown from net portfolio close NAV versus the running close-NAV high-water mark. If drawdown crosses a frozen threshold at a close, cut risky exposure from 60% to 30% at the following session's open; keep it at 30% until close drawdown recovers to half the trigger, then restore to 60% at the next open. This hysteresis avoids resetting the position repeatedly around one boundary.

Test only three trigger values, -8%, -10%, and -12%, with recovery thresholds -4%, -5%, and -6%, respectively. These are a compact sensitivity band around the practitioner example's -10% floor, not claimed optimal values. Do not tune the band on the full history. Carry real whole-unit holdings, pro-rata reduce both ETF sleeves, apply the existing instrument-specific fees and the same 50 bps per-side slippage (with 100 bps as a stress), credit only the currently supported NIFTYBEES cash distributions, and terminally liquidate. Run the same conservative high-before-low OHLC envelope and compare turnover, fee/slippage drag, CAGR, close drawdown, and bootstrap breach frequency with both fixed controls.

The trigger is a **risk response, not a hard loss guarantee**: a gap between the signal close and next-open execution can cross the loss limit before exposure is reduced. Also inspect whether the model uses the previous close mark for drawdown and only changes exposure on the next open; intraday lows must not feed a same-session signal.

## Advancement and validation gates

Keep all three thresholds as a reported frontier. Advance none unless a pre-declared chronological replay passes the conservative 20% OHLC screen, has net results after current fees and both slippage settings, and improves the risk/return trade-off versus fixed 45% without relying on the already-inspected 2020–2026 history to select its threshold. Report bootstrap breach estimates beside historical drawdowns; do not interpret them as forecasts or guarantees. Because existing history has been inspected, only observations collected after a frozen paper specification can serve as fresh out-of-sample evidence. The current paper protocol begins 2026-10-28; any later addition should be separately frozen before its first observation and must not place broker orders.

The source work treats drawdown control as an allocation discipline, not as a way to guarantee a market-price stop under discrete observations. Transaction costs, market gaps, ETF price/NAV differences, NIFTY distribution-unit mapping, and incomplete GOLDBEES distribution history remain binding implementation limits.
